## Body diagram geometry

The muscle outlines the body maps are drawn from (`assets/data/body_paths.json`, generated by
`tool/gen_body_paths.mjs`) are derived from [MuscleMap](https://github.com/melihcolpan/MuscleMap)
by Melih Colpan, used under the **MIT License**. The paths were converted from Swift source to
JSON, sub-group shapes were dropped, and nothing else about the artwork was changed.

## Exercise data & media

The exercise names, instructions and animations (`assets/data/exercises.json`, `assets/instr/`,
`assets/img/`, `assets/gif/`) come from
[hasaneyldrm/exercises-dataset](https://github.com/hasaneyldrm/exercises-dataset) and are **not**
covered by this project's AGPL licence — they remain under that dataset's own terms. The media
files are not distributed in this repository; they are fetched by `tool/sync_media.sh`. If you
redistribute myOpenGym with the media included, review the upstream licence first.

## Food data

The food catalogue (`assets/data/foods.json`, generated by `tool/gen_foods.mjs` from the curated
list in `tool/foods_seed.tsv`) is derived from
[USDA FoodData Central](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/), SR Legacy release 2021-10-28.

FoodData Central is a work of the United States federal government and is in the **public
domain** — there is no attribution requirement and no share-alike condition. It is credited here
because knowing where a nutrition number came from is worth more than the licence is: every
entry keeps its source record id in its `src` field, so any figure in the app can be checked
against the USDA record it was taken from.

Which foods appear is this project's own curation and is covered by the AGPL like the rest of
the code. Open Food Facts was considered and not used: its data is ODbL and its images are
CC-BY-SA, and neither obligation belongs in a bundled mobile binary.

## Food photography

The photographs in `assets/food/` are **not** ours. Each one is an individual work fetched from
[Openverse](https://openverse.org/) — overwhelmingly from Wikimedia Commons — and each keeps
the licence it arrived with. Most are **CC BY** or **CC BY-SA**; some are **CC0** or carry the
**Public Domain Mark**. Non-commercial (NC) licences are never accepted.

Every image is listed in [`tool/food_media.tsv`](tool/food_media.tsv) with its licence, its
creator and the page it came from, resolved by `tool/fetch_food_media.mjs` and downloaded by
`tool/sync_food_media.sh`. The creator and licence are also copied into `assets/data/foods.json`
and shown under the photograph in the app, because attribution has to travel with the work
rather than sit in a file on a repository host.

Public domain alone was tried first and abandoned. The CC0 pool for food photography is thin
and noisy — a search for "bacon" returns busts of Francis Bacon, and "beef chuck" returns a
Chuck E. Cheese sign — and about a third of what it produced was the wrong subject. A wrong
photograph is worse than none, since a food with no picture falls back to a drawn category
glyph and is at least honest about it. Foods with no confident match keep that fallback.

The images are modified: each is centre-cropped and re-encoded to a 512x512 JPEG so the
catalogue reads as one grid. For the share-alike images, that crop is a derivative and carries
the same licence as its original.

As with the exercise media, these files are **not distributed in this repository** — they are
git-ignored and fetched on demand. If you redistribute myOpenGym with them included, the
per-image attribution in the manifest is what you need to carry with them.
